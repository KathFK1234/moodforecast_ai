Privacy

Last updated 16 November 2026.

You can check the weather without giving any details. Places you search for are sent to our weather and map services, and your browser remembers the last one.

Use my location

Your location is only used when you tap the location button next to the search, and only after your browser asks for your permission. The page rounds your position to about 1 km before sending it to our site, which asks OpenStreetMap's Nominatim for the town there and then shows that town's weather like any search. Your position isn't saved, and it isn't in our request logs, because it travels inside the request rather than in the web address. The rounded point is kept in the server's memory for up to 10 minutes, not linked to you, so asking again is quick. The town's name is then handled like any place you search for. You can turn off location access for this site in your browser's settings.

The map

The map loads only when you open it. Its code comes from this site, but the map pictures come straight from OpenStreetMap's servers to your browser, as on any site with an OpenStreetMap map. So OpenStreetMap sees your network address, which part of the map you're looking at, and that you came from this site (see their privacy policy). When you tap the map, the point is handled as for "Use my location": rounded to about 1 km, not saved, and not in our logs.

Activity picks that learn what you like

When you tap 👍 or 👎 under a suggested activity, your browser keeps the vote, the activity and the kind of weather it was in (warm, mild or cold; dry or wet; day or evening), so later suggestions suit you better. Older votes count for less over time. This is kept only in your browser's storage on this device, and your votes are never sent to us. When the page suggests something it has learned you like, it asks the site for that activity by name, just as when you ask about one yourself, so the site sees the activity but not why. To clear it, choose "Forget what I've liked" under a suggestion, or clear this site's data in your browser. The mood score is never adjusted to you: it comes from the weather alone.

Daily email alerts

If you subscribe, we keep your email address, your location and your chosen activity, and use them only to send your alerts. Your browser remembers the subscription too.

Who handles it

The site runs on Railway, and Brevo sends the emails. Searches go to Open-Meteo and OpenStreetMap. The pages describing the site's API for developers (/docs and /redoc) load their code from jsDelivr, and ReDoc's logo from Redocly; the rest of the site loads nothing from other sites, apart from the map's pictures from OpenStreetMap once you open the map. These services operate in other countries, so your details may be handled outside the one you're in. We don't sell your details or use them for advertising.

How long we keep it

  • Unsubscribing deletes your details from our database straight away.
  • A request nobody confirms is deleted after 48 hours.
  • Brevo keeps its own record of emails it has sent for a limited time.
  • Railway logs requests to the site, including your network address and the places you search for, for a limited time.
  • Limits on repeated use keep your network address, and any email address typed into a form, in memory for up to a day. They aren't saved.

Questions
